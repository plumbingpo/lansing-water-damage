Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at Lansing Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted

Our Structural Drying Process

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Equipment Deployment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

A technician comes back every day, reads moisture at each monitoring point, moves equipment as needed and logs the progress. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Lansing

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
