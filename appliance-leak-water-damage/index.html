Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Lansing

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Lansing crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the Machine DownStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage, so slow seepage at a dishwasher or an ice maker line makes for a tougher claim. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
  • A kinked or cracked ice maker line, or one whose fitting has worked loose.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Kitchen Cabinets

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.

How We Fix It

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Move the Appliance and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Extract the Water

Standing water is extracted from floors and carpet. Where water is trapped under cabinets or floating floors, we remove toe kicks or lift boards to reach it.

3

Save Materials by Drying

Our crew places drying equipment and aims airflow at the hardwood and the cabinets, drying them in place whenever possible to avoid removing them.

4

Sanitize and Take Final Readings

Any surface that dishwasher or washer discharge reached is treated with an antimicrobial. Before the appliance is put back, a last set of readings confirms the floor under it is no longer damp.
