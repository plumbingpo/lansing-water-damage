The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Lansing crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. They are two phases. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. First is source control, which keeps more water from coming in. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Containment, using plastic barriers and controlled airflow so moisture and contaminants stay out of clean rooms. Four: stabilize by pulling materials beyond saving, applying antimicrobial to surfaces and setting up air movers plus dehumidifiers. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

Why Professional Water Damage Mitigation Matters

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? Does the cabinet toe kick need to come off? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Under most policies you must act reasonably to keep the damage from growing, with proof that you did. Arrival photos, daily moisture readings and an inventory of removed materials all go into our records. That file shows what the water did and what was done about it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

How Our Water Damage Mitigation Works

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. Should a lasting fix require a plumber or a roofer, our team coordinates when that happens.

2

Extract the Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Contain Wet Areas

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. As a result, contaminants, dust and humid air stay put instead of spreading around the house mid-job.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. Moisture readings are recorded on every visit until the structure can be repaired, typically in 3 to 5 days.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A monthly look around toilets and inside sink cabinets catches drips, staining and spongy flooring.
  • An automatic valve on the main line can cut the water as soon as it detects a leak.
  • Leave the heat on during winter trips so the plumbing cannot freeze.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Mitigation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Underfoot the carpet is spongy because the padding below holds water.
  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.
  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.
